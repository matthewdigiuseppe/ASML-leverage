# ASML, EUV and export controls: fact-checked strand memo (as of 28 October 2026)

Strand: ASML and the lithography chokepoint, facts through October 2026.
Checked against primary documents wherever possible: ASML SEC filings (20-F for FY2019, FY2022, FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025; quarterly 6-K press releases, investor presentations and US GAAP statements from 2023 to Q3 2026), the Federal Register (govinfo text and the federalregister.gov API), Congress.gov API, Dutch Staatscourant and Kamerstukken (officielebekendmakingen.nl), and EU Cellar metadata for EUR-Lex acts. News items were checked by fetching the cited page. The session's web-search budget was exhausted before this check began, so items whose pages were blocked (Reuters, CNBC, SCMP, Bloomberg, Taylor Wessing, US News, DatacenterDynamics) could not be confirmed and are marked accordingly.

## Synthesis (edited)

**Position 1. The chokepoint is real, but the leverage is jointly and unevenly held, and the Netherlands controls the smallest share of the legal and coercive tools around it.** ASML is the only EUV supplier. It recognized 48 EUV systems in 2025 (44 NXE, 4 High-NA EXE). In July 2026 the CFO said ASML expects to ship around 65 Low-NA EUV systems in 2026 and that 2027 Low-NA EUV is "close to being fully covered with orders"; in October 2026 the CEO said planned supply for 2027 and 2028 "will not fully meet the demand of our customers." The machine is transnational. Carl Zeiss SMT is ASML's sole supplier of lenses, mirrors, illuminators, collectors and other critical optics under an exclusive arrangement, and ASML's 20-F says that if Zeiss stopped supplying for a prolonged period ASML "would effectively cease to be able to conduct our business." ASML owns 24.9% of Carl Zeiss SMT Holding. San Diego (Cymer) is ASML's light-source center of excellence, and the EUV source is driven by TRUMPF's CO2 laser amplifier. ASML manufactures in Wilton and San Diego and had 8,242 US employees at end-2025 (18.7% of 44,175). It also manufactures in Linkou, Tainan and Pyeongtaek and runs repair centres in Hwaseong, Linkou, Beijing, Wilton, San Diego, Vancouver (WA) and Veldhoven. This geography gives Washington jurisdictional hooks (US-origin content, US persons, US sites). It also means part of the "European" lever is German and part of the "Dutch" firm is American. A model that gives The Hague a unilateral veto over EUV overstates its hand. A model in which Washington can redirect ASML output at low cost understates the US cost, because the firms that would bear it (TSMC, Samsung, SK Hynix, Intel) are US partners.

**Position 2. Since 2019 the Netherlands has acted more as a delayer than a decider.** ASML's application to ship an EUV system to China was still pending in October 2020, when the trade minister gave parliament a confidential annex on its status. Press reports attribute the delay to US lobbying from 2018 onward; that account could not be independently confirmed here. National DUVi controls were published on 30 June 2023 and took effect on 1 September 2023 (NXT:2000i and later). At the end of 2023 the government partially revoked a licence for NXT:2050i and NXT:2100i shipments to a small number of Chinese customers. On 7 September 2024 NXT:1970i and 1980i licensing moved from Washington to The Hague, including specially designed parts, software and technology. Metrology and inspection controls were published on 15 January 2025 and entered into force on 1 April 2025. In November 2025 the EU folded the Dutch controls into the Union dual-use list (Delegated Regulation 2025/2003, in force 15 November 2025). Each step followed a US move (October 2022, October 2023, December 2024), and the Dutch kept the form (national licensing) and some control over timing. The December 2024 BIS rule created Supplement No. 4 to Part 742, which lists the Netherlands, 24 other EU member states (all but Cyprus and Malta), Japan, the UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Norway, Iceland and Switzerland, but not South Korea or Taiwan. Exporters located there are excluded from the new SME foreign direct product licence requirements. BIS justified the list by regime membership and the authority to control key SME items; a separate exclusion covers countries that have "implemented equivalent controls." Because BIS can amend the supplement by rule, the exclusion is a revocable status. The MATCH Act (H.R. 8170) would add a 150-day clock for allied alignment, backed by unilateral US controls and an expanded FDPR. The 2026 MATCH dispute is the most explicit Dutch objection on the public record: the prime minister raised it at the White House on 13 April, the trade minister objected in written answers to parliament on 12 May, and he lobbied in Washington in June. The House Foreign Affairs Committee ordered the bill reported 36 to 8 on 22 April 2026. Its fate depends on the FY2027 NDAA, which remained unresolved as of October 2026, so this is the live test of whether resistance is possible.

**Position 3. The direction of exposure matters more than the scenario implies.** US-sited customers took 12.5% of ASML's total net sales in 2025 and 13% of net system sales in Q3 2026, against 32% for Taiwan and 31% for South Korea. US dependence on ASML runs mainly through TSMC in Taiwan and Korean memory makers, not through Arizona. A European threat to restrict servicing of US-sited fabs bites on a small but salient revenue base. Withholding from all US-aligned fabs would mean withholding from ASML's largest customers: four customers took 61.2% of 2025 sales, and non-current contract liabilities (payments received ahead of performance) rose from EUR 3.4bn at end-2025 to EUR 9.3bn at 27 September 2026. Carrying out that threat would be very costly to ASML and the Dutch economy, which weakens its credibility.

**Position 4. In 2026 the live lever is servicing, not new sales, and its value against China is wasting.** Installed Base Management was 25.1% of 2025 total net sales and reached EUR 3.3bn (28.2% of sales) in Q3 2026. AEI estimates that China runs "several hundred" DUVi tools that need specialized maintenance about every six months, often provided on site by ASML. China's share of net system sales was 8% in Q1 2023, then 46% to 49% from Q3 2023 through Q3 2024, as Dutch and US DUV controls were announced and phased in. It fell from 36% (Q4 2025) to 19% (Q1 2026) and 14% (Q2 2026), then rose to 23% in Q3 2026. ASML guided China to about 20% of 2026 total net sales, "mainly related to an increased demand in mainstream Logic." The 2023 to 2024 surge is consistent with an anticipation effect: an expected cutoff pulls purchases forward, which enriches ASML now and lowers the value of a later cutoff. Press claims that executives attributed the Q3 2026 rebound to three-year stockpiling by Chinese memory makers could not be verified, so stockpiling remains a hypothesis to model, not an observed motive. If anticipation operates, every quarter of delay moves bargaining power away from the denial coalition. That is the inability-to-commit problem in concrete form, and it explains why Washington wants deadlines and ASML wants time.

**Position 5. China has credible counter-levers that act on ASML directly.** ASML says it began preparing for Chinese rare-earth restrictions in early 2024, focusing on magnets used in its systems, and has seen no material customer impact. China's expanded rare-earth controls of 9 October 2025 are suspended until 10 November 2026 under the Busan deal. In September 2026 the two governments agreed politically to extend the truce to 10 January 2027, but as of 13 October BIS had not amended its own suspension, and no such BIS rule had appeared in the Federal Register by 27 October. State Council Decree No. 835 (13 April 2026) creates a Malicious Entity List, prohibition orders, private damages suits and fines against those who implement identified foreign extraterritorial measures; the first MOFCOM blocking order (2 May 2026) targeted US sanctions on five Chinese petrochemical firms. Because ASML runs a repair centre in Beijing, ASML staff in China who implemented a US servicing ban could be exposed to these tools. Nexperia calibrates Dutch resolve. The economy minister issued an order under the Goods Availability Act on 30 September 2025, China imposed a Nexperia-specific export control on 4 October, and the minister suspended the order on 19 November 2025, telling parliament the suspension would help diplomatic talks. The Enterprise Chamber's measures (suspension of the CEO, shares placed under a court-appointed administrator) remained in place. The record shows quick retreat on the ministerial instrument under targeted Chinese retaliation, but not full capitulation.

**Position 6. US credibility is also imperfect, and the scenario underweights this.** Washington suspended its own Affiliates Rule for one year from 10 November 2025 as part of the Busan deal, with the rule written to snap back on 10 November 2026; in September 2026 an unnamed US official said the suspension would be extended with the truce. Export controls have become bargaining chips in US-China dealmaking. If the hegemon trades its own controls, allied compliance looks like a transfer to Washington's bilateral bargaining rather than a contribution to collective security. This is the context for the Dutch trade minister's June 2026 remark that MATCH "could potentially put our national security in American hands."

**Position 7. Europe 2031's stockpile claim is partly plausible but should be derived in the model, not assumed.** Bloomberg reported in May 2024, citing anonymous sources, that ASML offers joint service contracts letting customers such as TSMC do some routine maintenance themselves, but also that EUV tools quickly stop working without ASML spare parts and that ASML told Dutch officials it could disable machines remotely. ASML runs repair centres on US and Taiwanese soil, and collectors and optics are Zeiss items. Every stockpiled tool is capacity that customers forgo while supply is sold out. The US outside option should therefore be treated as finite: a survival time T without ASML that depends on stockpile investment.

**Alternatives do not break the EUV monopoly before 2031.** Reuters reported in December 2025 that a Shenzhen EUV prototype generates EUV light but had not produced working chips; the government target is 2028, insiders say 2030, and Zeiss-class optics are a major obstacle. Canon's nanoimprint tool went to the Texas Institute for Electronics for R&D and prototyping. xLight holds a finalized USD 150m CHIPS award (2 June 2026) for a free-electron-laser EUV source, with no verified prototype date. DUV multipatterning is eroding the DUV lever: SMIC makes 7 nm chips on ASML immersion tools with weak yields, and Hua Hong's Huali unit is preparing 7 nm at Fab 6 in Shanghai. The model should treat EUV leverage as durable over the scenario horizon and DUV and servicing leverage as decaying.

## Corrections and items dropped

- HFAC vote was 36 to 8 (Congress.gov), not 44 to 0.
- Metrology controls entered into force on 1 April 2025; the 20-F's "effective January 15, 2025" is the publication date.
- The December 2024 exclusion is a named-country list justified by regime membership and SME control authority. "Equivalent controls" is a separate, additional exclusion.
- The 33% (2025), 36% (Q4 2025) and 19% (Q1 2026) China figures are shares of net system sales, not total sales. The roughly 20% 2026 guidance is of total net sales.
- NL Times does not say Sjoerdsma met Lutnick or members of Congress; it says he met his "American counterpart."
- The Q3 2026 "three-year stockpiling by memory makers" attribution was not found in the cited source (XenoSpectrum) and is dropped.
- Dropped as unverifiable: TSMC's 56% share of the EUV installed base (SCMP blocked); Fouquet's "10-15 years behind" and "what is right for the Netherlands" quotes (2024); Dassen's 14 October 2026 MATCH comments (US News unavailable); Substrate's funding and 2028 target; Intel Fab 52 tool counts and TSMC Arizona fab 2 timing; "about five domestic Chinese DUVi tools in 2026"; "Dutch annual servicing licences since end-2024"; the regional split of ASML supplier spend; the detailed account of US lobbying in 2019 (Reuters, January 2020, blocked); the internal ASML email to US staff (CNBC, October 2022, blocked); whether MOFCOM formally extended its rare-earth suspension date.

## Modeling implications

1. Split control over the chokepoint. Model the lever as jointly held: NL/ASML holds physical supply and servicing; the US holds legal jurisdiction (FDPR, de minimis, US-person rules) and physical assets (Cymer San Diego, Wilton, 18.7% of ASML staff); Germany/Zeiss is a veto player inside the chokepoint as sole optics supplier. An EU or middle-power coalition matters only if it includes Germany.
2. Destination-weighted payoffs for NL/ASML. China: 13.8% (2022), 26.3% (2023), 36.1% (2024), 29.1% (2025), about 20% guided for 2026 of total net sales. US: 9.4% to 16.0%. Taiwan plus Korea: 38% to 67% (2022 to 2025). Withholding from "the US" in practice means withholding from Taiwan and Korea, so the cost of executing an anti-US threat is very high.
3. Make the conditional exclusion explicit. The December 2024 rule names the Netherlands, Japan and most EU states in Supplement No. 4; BIS can amend that list by rule. Model the US offer as a revocable status (comply and keep jurisdictional autonomy, defy and face FDPR). The MATCH Act's 150-day clock is a deadline-based commitment device.
4. Dutch behavior since 2019 fits a "delayer" equilibrium: concede on substance, keep national form and some control of timing (pending EUV licence in 2020; national DUVi rules 2023; re-nationalized 1970i/1980i licensing 2024; three-month lag between publication and force in 2025). A war-of-attrition or timing game in which NL chooses when, not whether, to comply, with per-period US pressure costs and a per-period ASML China profit flow, fits this record.
5. Wasting asset and anticipation. China's share of system sales jumped from 8% (Q1 2023) to 46-49% (Q3 2023 to Q3 2024) while controls were being phased in. Let the value of a future DUV or servicing cutoff V_t fall with China's stockpile S_t and indigenization I_t, so delay transfers power to China and the denial threat is front-loaded. Treat stockpiling as an equilibrium response to be derived, since the Q3 2026 stockpiling attribution is unverified.
6. Separate levers by durability. EUV denial is durable through 2031 (Chinese EUV chips not before 2028-2030 per Reuters' sources; Canon NIL and xLight are R&D-stage). DUV and servicing leverage decay with Chinese DUV multipatterning (SMIC, Huali).
7. Servicing is the live margin. Installed Base Management rose from 20.4% (2023) to 25.1% (2025) and 28.2% (Q3 2026) of total net sales. Model servicing as a repeated action that can be withdrawn later, so both the US and China can condition on it. It gives NL a continuous lever but makes ASML staff and the Beijing repair centre exposed under Decree 835.
8. Chinese counter-coercion has two channels: rare-earth supply (a suspension with a fixed expiry, so a reusable threat) and legal penalties on firms that implement foreign extraterritorial measures. Nexperia (30 Sept to 19 Nov 2025) shows rapid Dutch retreat on the ministerial order under targeted retaliation, with court-ordered measures left in place, so resolve should be modeled as instrument-specific rather than all-or-nothing.
9. US credibility as private information. The US suspended its own Affiliates Rule in the Busan deal and signaled an extension in September 2026, so allies are uncertain whether the US is a security hawk or transactional. A signaling or screening setup in which allied compliance is worth less if the US may trade controls with Beijing fits the evidence and the Dutch rhetoric about "national security in American hands."
10. Stockpile outside option. Parameterize T_US, the time US-aligned fabs can run without ASML service and spares, as an investment whose cost is forgone capacity while supply is sold out through 2028, and compare it with T_EU, Europe's endurance without US frontier AI. Europe 2031 assumes T_US > T_EU; the model should derive when this holds.
11. Issue linkage with defense. The record shows Dutch concessions on export controls without explicit compensation. Let US security guarantees enter as an implicit side payment or threat, which may explain compliance without a visible quid pro quo.

## Open questions

- Will the MATCH Act survive the FY2027 NDAA conference, and in what form (servicing ban, countrywide DUVi ban, 150-day clock, waiver authority)?
- Will BIS publish an extension of the Affiliates Rule suspension before 10 November 2026, and will MOFCOM formally extend its rare-earth suspension to 10 January 2027?
- What is the true size of ASML's China installed base by tool type and its China servicing revenue? ASML does not disclose either; the USD 1-1.5bn and "more than 300 DUVi" figures are unattributed press estimates.
- What drove the Q3 2026 rebound in China's share (23% of system sales): pull-forward ahead of MATCH, multi-year stockpiling, or mainstream-logic demand as ASML says?
- How long can EUV and DUVi tools run without OEM service and spare parts (collector lifetimes, Zeiss optics refurbishment, Cymer source consumables), and how much maintenance capacity do TSMC and Samsung have in house?
- What share of a current NXE or EXE tool is US-origin by value under EAR de minimis rules, and could the US credibly apply FDPR to ASML's sales to TSMC or Samsung, not just to China?
- Does the Netherlands license servicing of restricted tools in China on a fixed renewal cycle, and on what terms? (The 2024 regulation covers specially designed parts, software and technology; the renewal cycle is unverified.)
- Is there evidence behind the June 2026 US claim that an EUV tool reached China, and has the Shenzhen prototype produced chips in 2026?
- Does ASML's 11.1% Mistral stake give the EU a usable link between lithography and access to frontier AI?
- What do the original Reuters reports (January 2020 on US lobbying; October 2022 on ASML's US staff) say exactly? Both should be checked manually before citation.
