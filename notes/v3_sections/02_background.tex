\section{The Chokepoint and Its Owners}\label{sec:background}

\subsection{A nested chokepoint}

ASML is the only maker of extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography machines, on which the leading-edge logic of the most advanced US AI accelerators depends \citep{miller2022chip, asml2026annual}. China, which cannot buy them, buys immersion deep-ultraviolet (DUV) tools, and \citet{aei2026lithography} estimate that Chinese entities took 70 percent of ASML's immersion DUV sales in 2024. Exposing a layer several times (multiple patterning), these tools print features that would otherwise need EUV. SMIC makes 7nm logic this way, the node of Huawei's Ascend accelerators, and without domestic EUV \citet{allen2025deepseek} expects China to stay there. Customers in China accounted for 26.3 percent of ASML's net sales in 2023, 36.1 percent in 2024 and 29.1 percent in 2025 \citep{asml2026annual}. Servicing follows each sale. Installed-base management made up 25.1 percent of ASML's 2025 sales \citep{asml2026annual}, and several hundred ASML immersion tools in China need specialized maintenance about every six months, often from ASML staff on site \citep{aei2026lithography}.

ASML's supply chain runs through Germany and the United States. Carl Zeiss SMT is its sole supplier of critical optics \citep{asml2026annual}. At the end of 2025 ASML had 8,242 employees in the United States, 18.7 percent of its workforce \citep{asml2026annual}, and US subsidiaries including Cymer, the maker of its EUV light sources \citep{asml2026subsidiaries}. Under US export rules these entities and their US staff are ``U.S. persons'' \citep{ecfr2026usperson}, subject to US rules that restrict support for advanced-node fabs in China \citep{asml2026annual}.

Washington also reaches the machines. An October 2023 rule set the de minimis threshold of US-origin content at zero for certain immersion tools bound for China \citep{ecfr7344, bis2023sme}. A rule effective on 2 December 2024 added a foreign direct product (FDP) rule and a de minimis rule specific to semiconductor manufacturing equipment (SME). Exporters in the 33 countries of its new Supplement No.~4 (25 EU members and Japan among them, but not South Korea or Taiwan) are excluded from the new licence requirements for items caught only by these two rules, because the listed governments ``have the authority to control key SME items of concern'' \citep{bis2024fdpr}. BIS can amend the list by rule (\cref{sec:coding} codes the status).

\subsection{The control sequence}

Licences for ASML's shipments to China are a Dutch decision (\cref{sec:gatekeeper}). The Hague licensed an EUV sale to China in 2018 but, after US lobbying in 2019 that Reuters reported, did not renew the expired licence; ASML's new application was still pending in October 2020 \citep{nltimes2020pressured, kaag2020asml}. From 2023 each step followed a US move. National licensing of the most advanced immersion tools (NXT:2000i and later) took effect on 1 September 2023, after the US rules of October 2022 \citep{govnl2023controls}. Licensing of the older NXT:1970i and NXT:1980i moved from Washington to The Hague on 7 September 2024, after the US rule of October 2023 \citep{stcrt2024duv, asml2024license}. Controls on metrology and inspection systems took effect on 1 April 2025, after the December 2024 rule \citep{stcrt2025metrology}. On 15 November 2025 Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/2003 put the most advanced immersion tools on the EU list \citep{eu2025dualuse}. ASML reported that this ``did not mean a change'': the licence decision stayed in The Hague \citep{asml2026annual}.

The US-facing direction is collective. EUV and the most advanced immersion tools ship to US fabs under the Union general export authorisation EU001, which a member state may withdraw from an exporter only for doubts about its ability to comply \citep{eu2021dualuse}. Only the NXT:1970i and NXT:1980i class, left on the Dutch national list, still need a Dutch licence to leave the EU \citep{stcrt2025_38653}. Turning the listed tools against Washington would take an EU decision (\cref{sec:council}).

ASML's ownership is guarded by a foundation that can acquire preference shares halving ordinary shareholders' votes \citep{asml2026annual} and by notification duties under the Vifo Act \citep{nl2022vifo, nl2023sensitief}.

Servicing in China remained permitted at least into mid-2026 \citep{zakaria2026match}. The MATCH Act, introduced in April 2026 and not enacted by late October, would ban immersion-tool sales anywhere in China and servicing at listed Chinese fabs, and would give allies 150 days to align before Washington acted alone \citep{baumgartner2026match, hr8170}. The Hague opposed the bill's extraterritorial reach \citep{tk2026match}; its trade minister said the bill ``could potentially put our national security in American hands'' \citep{nltimes2026irritated}.

\subsection{Europe's dependencies}

\paragraph{Security.} Europe relies on US nuclear deterrence, and replacing US conventional support would cost about EUR 250 billion a year \citep{burilkov2025defending}. The guarantee is graduated. Its core is statutory: Section 1250A of the FY2024 National Defense Authorization Act bars withdrawal from the North Atlantic Treaty without two-thirds of the Senate or an Act of Congress \citep{usc1928f}. Posture is cheap to move. On 1 May 2026, after Chancellor Merz criticized US strategy on Iran, Defense Secretary Hegseth ordered about 5,000 troops out of Germany \citep{martinez2026germany}. Dependence also runs the other way. On 30 March 2026 Spain, already refusing US use of jointly operated bases for the Iran war, closed its airspace to the US aircraft involved \citep{roque2026spain}.

\paragraph{Frontier AI.} The AI Diffusion Rule of January 2025 put ten EU members in its favoured group and held the other seventeen to country allocations \citep{bis2025diffusion}. Two days before compliance was due, BIS announced its rescission, citing the ``second-tier status'' the rule would have given dozens of countries \citep{bis2025rescission}; by late October 2026 neither the formal rescission nor a replacement had been published. In the August 2025 joint statement the EU said it intended to buy at least \$40 billion of US AI chips and to align its technology-security requirements with US ones, and Washington said it would then facilitate those exports \citep{whitehouse2025jointstatement}. The National Security Strategy of December 2025 holds out ``more favorable treatment on commercial matters, technology sharing, and defense procurement'' to countries that take more responsibility for their regional security and ``align their export controls with ours'' \citep{whitehouse2025nss}.

\paragraph{China.} Beijing has built instruments against those who restrict it. Its licensing of seven medium and heavy rare earths, in place since 4 April 2025, is still in force \citep{mofcom2025rareearth, benchmark2026rareearth}. Its broader, extraterritorial controls of October 2025 \citep{mayerbrown2025rareearth} were suspended until 10 November 2026 after the Busan summit of 30 October 2025 \citep{whitehouse2025busan, mofcom2025pause}. In September 2026 the two governments agreed to extend their truce to 10 January 2027, but we found no Chinese act extending the suspension itself \citep{mofcom2026extension}; \cref{sec:tests} asks what a lapse would bring. China's 2021 blocking rules, first applied on 2 May 2026 against US sanctions on five refiners, already let it penalize compliance with foreign extraterritorial measures \citep{squire2026blocking, geopolitechs2026blocking}. Two State Council decrees of April 2026 widened those powers, authorizing countermeasures against foreign firms that cut off normal trade with Chinese entities (Decree 834) and penalties for those who implement foreign extraterritorial measures (Decree 835) \citep{statecouncil2026supplychain, reedsmith2026decree}. Decree 835 was first used against Brussels. On 15 May 2026 Beijing barred anyone from implementing the Commission's investigative measures against Nuctech under the Foreign Subsidies Regulation \citep{cleary2026nuctech, mofcom2026nuctech}.
