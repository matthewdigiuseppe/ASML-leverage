- **Council record (sec:council and sec:holdup):** member states gave positive opinions to US-facing countermeasure packages on 9 April 2025 and 24 July 2025 (Regulation 2025/1564); neither applied; each suspension followed a US move (90-day pause; Turnberry; Greenland withdrawal), and the July 2026 suspension (2026/1893) has no end date: "arm, then suspend on a US concession". The binding constraint is Commission gatekeeping: recital 85 of the ACI and Statement C/2023/1341 commit the Commission to avoid "going against any predominant position amongst Member States". Urgent, immediately applicable acts are available only to suspend or amend response measures (Art. 12(5)): de-escalation is fast and in the Commission's hands; escalation is slow and committee-gated. **Link to Corollary 5:** a European interruption decided through the ACI cannot be committed round by round before offers; its institutional timing resembles the after-offer timing under which interruption is never used. This is a sharp institutional implication for sec:holdup and sec:implications.

## Model remark on Chinese punishment
- The record shows China punishing specific acts: the Dutch Goods Availability Act order (Nexperia), Japan's Taiwan statement, the Commission's FSR probe of Nuctech (Decree 835 determination), European defence firms. We find no punishment of a holder for aligning its chokepoint export controls with Washington. In the model, punishment of specific acts is a counter-threat on a concession (Proposition 3); punishment of a security statement with a demonstration motive is self-rewarding (g_r >= 0) and would enter Q only if attached to alignment itself.
