\section{Europe 2031 Read Through the Model}\label{app:scenario}

\emph{Europe 2031} stages four rounds of US--European bargaining over ASML \citep{juijn2026europe}. Its authors say the storyline ``is not meant to be a prediction'', and we do not treat it as evidence. With as many free parameters as our model has, the statement that an episode is an equilibrium carries little information on its own. \Cref{tab:scenario} therefore states, for each move, the result that applies and what the move assumes about the parameters.

\paragraph{2028.} Washington threatens the foreign direct product rule to end ASML's remaining immersion sales and servicing in China; ``The Dutch cave'' and get nothing in return \citep{juijn2026europe}. The demand concerns the hedge, and the threat uses flow jurisdiction, which by \cref{prop:counter}(b) is credible whenever its net ex post cost is within Washington's stake, whatever Europe's slack. The text concedes that ``such penalties would also harm the US''. In our model that self-harm attaches to penalties on the whole firm ($\chi_f$), and jurisdiction over China-bound flows stays credible for every $V$. The episode needs a nonpositive premium and no effective rupture risk. The crisis comes during a compute crunch, when $V$ is high, so if the dispute carried a hazard of rupture and the Dutch could make proposals, \cref{cor:master}(c) would give Europe a positive transfer whenever $Q+\beta(V-Q)>0$, unless Washington's credible conditional threats clawed it back (\cref{rem:rupture}). The scenario attributes the outcome to a European position that ``fragments before it has properly formed''. Licensing is a Dutch decision (\cref{sec:gatekeeper}), and partners reach the gatekeeper's premium only through transfers, so a united position would not by itself have changed $Q$.

\paragraph{2029.} The Frontier Inference Services Rule caps Tier~2 countries at 25 percent of any provider's frontier inference, which the text says means ``roughly halving European allocations'', while the Netherlands joins the Five Eyes, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan in Tier~1 \citep{juijn2026europe}. Cutting a loyal Europe lowers the conditional part of the AI instrument, because conditional parts are measured from what a loyal ally receives (\cref{sec:security}). Washington spends slack to keep compute, and Europe's premium drifts upward. We read the withdrawal of the real AI Diffusion Rule before compliance was due (\cref{sec:background}) as a sign that such tiering is costly for Washington. Tier~1 for the Netherlands is the targeted benefit of \cref{rem:gatekeeper}.

The failed vote on the Arizona measure rests on the thinnest assumptions. The states the scenario names as opposing or abstaining cannot block the Council's determination (\cref{sec:council} gives the arithmetic and the completions), and what fails is a response measure, which after appeal only a qualified majority against can stop \citep{eu2023aci, eu2011comitology}. The story needs more members with $q_i\le 0$ than it names, or a Commission that will not go against a predominant position among member states \citep{commission2023acistatement}. We think the second is likelier, though it points to a measure that is never tabled, and the Union's record against Washington is to arm countermeasures and suspend them on a US concession (\cref{sec:council}). Even if adopted, the measure, a hold-up instrument aimed at the servicing Washington can most easily compel (\cref{sec:holdup}), could be lifted after a US offer, the timing under which \cref{cor:interrupt} gives no interruption.

\paragraph{The stockpile.} Washington's claim that through Taiwanese partners it has ``amassed enough EUV machines and maintenance know-how'' to outlast Europe \citep{juijn2026europe} is a claim about $t_U$ and $h$ in \cref{cor:interrupt}. It lowers $c_S$, what Washington's own fabs would lose in a dispute, and leaves $V$, its loss if China gained the tools, where it was. The claim and the ultimatum are therefore compatible: a Washington that can do without ASML's servicing still has a motive to keep China from gaining the chokepoint. Under complete information the claim would be either true or irrelevant; as a bluff it belongs to the incomplete-information extension that \cref{sec:limits} sets aside.

\paragraph{2031.} The vice president's offer is a preventive demand of the kind \cref{prop:control} describes, made ``now, while the option still exists'' \citep{juijn2026europe}. Before it, Chinese state-backed funds extend credit to a Portuguese infrastructure bank, refinance Greek sovereign debt and underwrite a Spanish regional government, and rationing has lowered $d_{ai}$. Rationing raises Europe's premium directly; the Chinese credit raises it only insofar as it shows what China could credibly promise for exit. A Washington that expects the premium to turn positive acts while its threat still bites (\cref{cor:windows}). A controlling vote in a Dutch-American holding stays under Dutch law and has low enforceability $\Delta_c$, which by \cref{cor:control}(c) lowers what control is worth to Washington. Capital for production on US soil raises $\Delta_c$. Cash transfers indexed to US AI windfalls are promises paid after control has passed, credible only up to $\bar m_U$ (\cref{sec:commitment}). If the Tier~3 threat is a lasting cut, \cref{lem:selflimit}(iii) gives it only its unconditional part as added bite at dates when Europe's premium is positive.

Since under complete information an issued ultimatum is accepted, Europe's refusal requires sovereignty costs that Washington cannot observe (\cref{prop:breakdown}). Beijing's warning on rare-earth terms is conditioned on signing, so by \cref{cor:control}(d) it is never carried out if control passes, and it protects European ownership only by deterring the demand. The scenario's further claim that signing with neither power means absorbing ``the full displeasure of both great powers at once'' needs a Chinese punishment of non-alignment that the text does not state.

The epilogue's counterfactual coalition of eight states, among them Japan and South Korea \citep{juijn2026europe}, lies outside the model, which has one rival and lets holders bargain one at a time (\cref{sec:limits}).
