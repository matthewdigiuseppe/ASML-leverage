\section{The Record as a Plausibility Probe}\label{sec:evidence}

The parameters of the model are not measured, and a negative exit premium can rationalize almost any act of compliance after the fact. Compliance also cannot separate coercion from shared threat perception, because successful coercion ends at the threat stage \citep{drezner2003hidden}. What follows is not a test (\cref{sec:tests}).

\subsection{Observable implications}\label{sec:predictions}

\Cref{tab:implications} sets six implications beside four rival accounts. Under shared threat perception the Netherlands aligns because it shares Washington's security concerns, as the cabinet says it does ``in general terms'' \citep[our translation]{tk2026match}; in the model that is a large intrinsic cost of exit $\eta$. In \citet{lake2009hierarchy} compliance is owed under a social contract in which the patron provides order and protection. In \citet{morrow1991alliances} the protégé trades autonomy for security. Under cost minimization, the fourth rival, a coercer prices each demand, one at a time, at a fixed cost of the cheapest credible instrument.

Under cost minimization the instruments used on one demand do not change the price of another, while in our model a concession on tariffs leaves less to extract on servicing. \subsection{Coding transfers and threats}\label{sec:coding}

Reading the record through \cref{prop:master} requires a line between a payment and a threat never carried out. We draw it at Washington's ex post optimal provision to an aligned ally, the baseline $x_j(A)$ of each instrument (\cref{sec:security}). A \emph{transfer} exceeds that baseline and enters $P^*$. A \emph{threat left unexercised} is a baseline benefit that Washington would withdraw after exit, or a sanction it could impose and does not. It enters the exit cost $c$ as a conditional part $d_j$, however valuable it is to the holder.

Because the baseline cannot be observed, we code a benefit as baseline provision when it is extended to allies that took no comparable step, justified by the recipient's own capacity or alliance status, and revocable by rule. We code it as a transfer when it is created in response to the holder's step, withheld from comparable allies and framed as compensation. Supplement No.~4 meets all three baseline criteria (\cref{sec:background}). We code it as a threat left unexercised, equivalently a reward for self-binding (\cref{rem:bind}), and code the favourable treatment that the National Security Strategy offers aligning countries the same way (\cref{sec:conditionality}).

The instrument behind a US demand is \emph{flow jurisdiction} if it reaches China-bound flows directly (de minimis and foreign direct product rules, entity listings, licences for US-origin items), \emph{conditional linkage} if it threatens to withdraw something in another domain that an exited holder would lose anyway (posture, AI access, trade terms), and \emph{unconditional} if it hurts the holder whatever it does. Chinese responses are counter-threats if announced before the holder concedes and executed if carried out.

Leverage in the model is the transfer, and on that measure the Netherlands has little to show almost by construction. The model also gives meaning to delay, to the scope of the holder's controls, to retained jurisdiction, which is self-binding with the key kept in Europe (\cref{rem:bind}), and to tolerated hedges. A tolerated hedge is rationing (\cref{cor:rationing}) if only linkage could stop it. It is a flow threat not yet credible if Washington could not stake the cost of blocking the flow itself (\cref{prop:counter}(b)).

\subsection{The Dutch record and Nexperia}\label{sec:record}

\paragraph{Alignment without a price.} Since 2019 The Hague has aligned in steps, from 2023 each following a US move (\cref{sec:background}), and under our rules it received no transfer. By \cref{cor:master}(b), the record is consistent with a premium that has been negative throughout if talks carry no risk of rupture or Washington makes the offers. The absence of payment identifies less than it seems to. Because $V$ is large for ASML, rupture risk combined with Dutch agenda power would imply a positive transfer unless $Q\le-\beta V/(1-\beta)$ (\cref{cor:master}(c)). And since $P^*$ approaches $\pos{Q}$ continuously as $\lambda\to 0$, a small payment cannot be told apart from none. Reading the record as $Q\le0$ therefore assumes that The Hague could not make demands whose rejection risked realignment, or that Washington's credible conditional threats took back what agenda power would have won (\cref{rem:rupture}).

The negative premium needs no security term. US flow jurisdiction over China-bound tools makes $\theta$ large, which shrinks both what China gains from exit and the relative gain it values, so $\omega$ is small unless China's cap binds (\cref{eq:omega}). ASML's US entities and staff are US persons (\cref{sec:background}), so the firm would not ship against a credible US penalty even if The Hague licensed exit (\cref{rem:firm}). With $\kappa=0$ these terms alone can make $Q$ negative. We have found no Chinese offer conditioned on Dutch exit, and ASML's revenue in China is not one. The record is therefore consistent with a jurisdictional source of Europe's slack and leaves the security channel largely untested. We found no documented case that links US security commitments to ASML, although public documents cannot exclude private linkage: the January 2023 talks behind the Dutch and Japanese controls were led by the US national security adviser and also covered Ukraine \citep{durbin2023biden}.

\paragraph{Orders of magnitude.} The terms of \cref{eq:Q} can be bounded, not estimated, and we give orders of magnitude in euros a year. The conditional part of the AI instrument, $d_{ai}$, is Europe's loss from moving from aligned to exited access to US frontier AI. It runs from about EUR 4 billion if European gains from AI are limited by adoption, through 50 billion, to 130 billion if they are limited by frontier capability \citep{acemoglu2024simple, filippucci2025g7, epoch2026eci}. The conditional part of the core guarantee, $\kappa S_k$, is about 12.5 billion at $\kappa=0.10$ and a stake of 125 billion, a value between costings of replacing US protection that leave out nuclear deterrence \citep{iiss2025defending, burilkov2025defending}; for $\kappa$ between 0.02 and 0.30 it runs from 2.5 to 37.5 billion. China's counter-threat of halting inputs to Europe runs from about 3 billion, our assumption for graduated punishment, to 77 billion, the ECB's estimated loss from a sudden halt of critical-dependency imports from the China-led bloc, applied to EU output \citep{attinasi2025hidden}. The midpoint of 15 billion is geometric, not an estimate.

If Washington's AI threat is credible, then, the AI term is likelier than the core guarantee to decide the sign of $Q$. China's counter-threat is not a term in $Q$, since China has punished specific acts and not alignment (\cref{sec:security}). It bears on which concessions Washington can extract (\cref{prop:counter}) and on China's punishment $r^c$ of a transfer of control (\cref{cor:control}). China's offer $\omega$ has no good measure. And $d_{ai}$ and $\kappa$ are not independent in practice, since US protection has responded to allies' conduct on unrelated issues (\cref{sec:background}).

\paragraph{Slack spent and unspent.} The sales controls came in steps from 2023, while servicing in China continued at least into mid-2026 (\cref{sec:background}). If the sales controls were backed by flow jurisdiction, as \cref{tab:cases} codes them, they drew on no slack (\cref{prop:counter}(b)), and the late arrival of demands on servicing reflects the cost of the flow threat. Rationing would require that some Dutch sales steps of 2023--2025 were backed by conditional linkage, which we have not found. The general linkage of AI exports and favourable treatment to export-control alignment came later, in August and December 2025 (\cref{sec:counter}), and names no Dutch step. Until 2026 Washington had no credible jurisdictional block on servicing done by ASML staff in China, who are not, we think, US persons, and since China's 2021 blocking rules and Decrees 834 and 835, stopping it carries a Chinese counter-threat (\cref{sec:background}). Timing does not separate coercion from shared threat perception either. A holder that shares Washington's view but would lose sales to Japanese rivals by moving alone would also wait for US rules that bind them, a coordination problem that \citet{farrell2019weaponized} identify when hubs lie in several jurisdictions.

\paragraph{Nexperia.} The Nexperia dispute of 2025 is the one episode in which China executed a counter-threat against a Dutch step taken under US pressure. The Dutch minutes of a meeting with the State Department on 12 June 2025 concluded that it was ``almost certain the CEO will have to be replaced to qualify for the exemption from the entity list'' \citep[paras.~2.19--2.20; our translation]{gerechtshof2025nexperia}, a Dutch record of US views, not a formal condition. On 30 September, the day after the Affiliates Rule took effect \citep{bis2025affiliatesrule}, the economy minister took control of Nexperia under the Goods Availability Act, and the Enterprise Chamber suspended its chief executive in early October \citep{kst32637720, gerechtshof2025nexperia}. On 4 October China announced an export control aimed at Nexperia's Chinese plants \citep{kst32637720}. At Busan on 30 October Washington agreed to suspend the Affiliates Rule for a year from 10 November, and China to ``take appropriate measures to ensure the resumption of trade from Nexperia's facilities in China'' \citep{fmprc2025busan, whitehouse2025busan, bis2025affiliates}. On 19 November the minister suspended his order, citing Chinese export licences for Nexperia chips. The Enterprise Chamber's separate measures stayed in force \citep{kst32637_717}.

We read the episode as a US-backed demand on the Netherlands met by an executed Chinese counter-threat. Under complete information, \cref{prop:counter}(a)(iii) allows execution on the path only when it crowds out another demand. That reading requires a Chinese threat known in The Hague and Washington before 30 September, a US demand that Washington shelved because the Nexperia concession had become expensive, and a Dutch government that conceded expecting to bear the cost. The alternative, outside the model, is that The Hague, and perhaps Washington, misjudged China's resolve. The reversal does not discriminate, since by then the rule behind the US demand had been suspended, and the public record establishes neither reading.

Europe bore the cost and was not compensated. By \cref{prop:counter}(a)(v) the counter-threat taxes the patron and not the holder only if Washington has a divisible sunk demand that China cannot reach. The relief came through a US concession to China at Busan, which we do not read as a transfer to Europe. What the episode would imply for the cost of compelling a firm is in \cref{sec:firm}.

\paragraph{Servicing, MATCH and Dutch politics.} A statute is a stake. The MATCH Act would make a flow threat against servicing credible if $e_F\le\bar m_U$, and such a threat leaves $Q$ unchanged and need not be carried out (\cref{prop:counter}(b)). The Dutch answer was voice (\cref{sec:background}), which is consistent both with jurisdiction alone keeping the premium negative and with perceived conditionality having risen after the targeted US moves of 2026. With $Q<0$, voice and the hold-up of \cref{sec:holdup} are all a holder has.

The seat arithmetic of two recent motions suggests which parts of that position would survive a change of government. On 13 October 2026 parliament adopted, 110 to 40, a motion co-signed by members of all three coalition parties that asks the cabinet to push for harmonized EU export control and for joint European action when third countries press a member state \citep{kst37020xvii11}. We read it as support for self-binding through Europeanized controls at a low sovereignty cost $\sigma^b$, and against a dual key (\cref{rem:bind}). On 22 January 2026 it had adopted, 103 to 46 and against the caretaker cabinet's advice, a motion to keep countermeasures, including the Anti-Coercion Instrument, in reserve over Greenland \citep{kst21501_20_2364}. Every right-leaning majority in the current house needs VVD, CDA, PVV, JA21 and the Markuszower group, and in practice BBB. Within that 78-seat bloc the first motion's supporters outnumber its opponents by 52 seats to 26, while the second's divide 40 to 38. On this arithmetic we expect Europeanized controls to survive a change of government and countermeasures against Washington to be at risk. Only 41 percent of Dutch respondents called the United States a reliable partner in spring 2026, against 88 percent in 2022 \citep{pew2026usimage}. No motion rejects MATCH, and Congress has not posted the stake: the bill has not moved since committee approval in April 2026 \citep{hr8170}, and neither chamber's FY2027 defence authorization contains it \citep{hr8800eh, s4784rs}.

\paragraph{What the comparison suggests.} We found no holder paid in cash for aligning on a chokepoint. The currency is status that BIS grants and withdraws by rule (Supplement No.~4, validated end-user status, annual licences), which we code as exit costs. Every holder in scope has, we think, a nonpositive premium, so the tables cannot test the threshold of \cref{cor:master}. They speak to the instruments Washington uses and to where holders resist. Where money moved, it moved to the patron (the offsets) or to a third party, as when Washington offered Brazil, not Bonn, a fuel guarantee in 1977 \citep{frus2015nonproliferation}.

We read Korea as flow jurisdiction rebuilt as a renewable status. BIS moved the Samsung and SK hynix fabs in China from validated end-user status to annual licences that allow operation but not expansion or upgrades \citep{afs2025veu, skhynix2026prospectus, reuters2025koreanlicence}, and the trade and security package of November 2025 did not mention export controls \citep{whitehouse2025korea}. The Japanese case is consistent with Chinese punishment aimed at a security act. After Prime Minister Takaichi told the Diet on 7 November 2025 that a Taiwan contingency could become a survival-threatening situation for Japan \citep{kokkai2025takaichi}, China placed forty Japanese entities on its export-control list and forty more on its watch list \citep{mofcom2026japan11, mofcom2026japan27}. None is among Japan's leading makers of lithography, deposition and etch tools or photoresists (Tokyo Electron, Nikon, Canon, JSR, Tokyo Ohka, Shin-Etsu). Beijing spared the holders whose inputs it needs. We found no US compensation \citep{teer2026dismantling}, which \cref{cor:master} leads us to expect while Japan's premium stays negative. Coordination among holders runs through Washington: Japan and South Korea have signed Pax Silica, the US-led initiative on AI chip supply chains and export controls, and TNW reported in June 2026 that the EU was set to join \citep{tnw2026paxsilica}.

Taiwan is consistent with what the model expects of a patron that can move production. TSMC's US commitment rose from about USD 12 billion in 2020 to USD 165 billion in 2025 \citep{tsmc2020arizona, tsmc2025expand}, and the control rights Washington has taken all attach to assets on US soil (\cref{sec:preventive}). We found no US demand for equity in a foreign chipmaking-equipment firm. A demand for a controlling vote in ASML presupposes that relocating EUV production and servicing is infeasible.

Resistance clusters on the installed base and on signed deals: Tokyo and The Hague kept servicing Chinese fabs, and Bonn kept its contract with Brazil. In the model these concessions cost the holder much relative to the remaining bite, but a cost minimizer facing expensive enforcement would also tolerate them.

Explicit links across issues appear in Johnson's tie between troops and offsets in 1966, in the posture cut in Germany and the threat to cut off trade with Spain after it denied base access in 2026 \citep{martinez2026germany, roque2026spain, omullony2026ankara}, and in the BIS rule of July 2026 that cited the UAE's role in Operation Epic Fury when it granted licence-free chip access \citep{bis2026uae}. None concerns a European holder's chip controls.

\paragraph{Where the comparison strains the model.} The offset payments were sunk concessions, which escape the slack cap (\cref{prop:slack}(b)), and renegotiation recurred from 1961 to 1967 \citep{frus1995foreignecon, frus1999germany, frus1995westerneurope}, as \cref{prop:slack}(d) leads us to expect of a patron whose stakes are small relative to its later demands. But a complete-information patron does not both collect and cut, and in 1967 Washington agreed to redeploy up to 35,000 personnel. If the troops were above Washington's ex post optimum, as balance-of-payments pressure and the Mansfield resolution suggest, keeping them was a transfer and the offsets were its price \citep{gavin2004gold, zimmermann2002money}. If not, the cut is unexplained, and FRUS does not let us choose. In 1957 Washington declined to back its China differential with linkage, as \cref{cor:rationing} implies for a demand worth little, but twelve of sixteen CoCom members backed abolition \citep{frus1989economicdefense, mastanduno1992economic}, a collective exit our single-holder model does not represent. In the Brazil case Bonn feared for a US commitment that Washington did not tie to its pressure \citep{frus2014westerneurope}, so conditionality existed only in the holder's beliefs.

\subsection{Designs that could test the model}\label{sec:tests}

\paragraph{An event dataset.} The unit would be a US demand on a holder of a chipmaking-equipment chokepoint (the Netherlands, Japan, South Korea, Germany) from 2019 to 2026, coded from gazettes, parliamentary records and filings for the instrument threatened (\cref{sec:coding}), the response, any compensation and any Chinese response. It should be built around the cross-issue budget. A concession in one issue area should lower what Washington extracts in another within the same period (\cref{prop:slack}(c), \cref{cor:rationing}). Linkage against hedges should fade as the premium rises toward zero, while flow jurisdiction does not track it (\cref{prop:counter}(b)).

\paragraph{An elite survey experiment.} Conditionality is a property of US behaviour, so the respondents should be US foreign-policy elites. Vignettes would vary whether an ally holding a chokepoint complied with US controls, refused, or complied on sales while servicing Chinese fabs. The gap in support for defending the ally and keeping forces there estimates $\kappa$ and $d_p$. Further arms would vary a public presidential stake and whether Washington uses bases there (\cref{prop:conditionality}).

\paragraph{Process tracing.} Requests under the Dutch Open Government Act (Woo) for records of export-control talks with US counterparts since 2019 would show whether security commitments, posture or the F-35 programme were raised. They would also date Dutch drafting against US demands, which separates coercion from shared threat perception, and show what The Hague expected from China before 30 September 2025.

\paragraph{The Busan expiry.} If the suspensions agreed at Busan lapse (\cref{sec:background}), the rule behind the Nexperia demand and China's extraterritorial rare-earth controls return together \citep{bis2025affiliates, mofcom2025pause}. \Cref{prop:counter}(a) then leads us to expect Washington to shift its demands on The Hague toward demands China cannot reach or flow jurisdiction it exercises itself. A second executed counter-threat against a Dutch step would be hard to square with misjudgment, which learning should correct.
