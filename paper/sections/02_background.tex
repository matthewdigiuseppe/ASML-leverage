\section{The Chokepoint and Its Owners}\label{sec:background}

\subsection{A chokepoint}

ASML is the only firm that makes extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography machines, on which the leading-edge logic processes used for the most advanced US AI accelerators depend \citep{miller2022chip, asml2026annual}. In October 2026 its chief executive said that planned EUV supply for 2027 and 2028 would not fully meet demand \citep{asml2026q3}.

China cannot buy EUV machines. It buys immersion deep-ultraviolet (DUV) tools instead, and \citet{aei2026lithography} estimate that Chinese entities took 70 percent of ASML's immersion DUV sales in 2024. Exposing the same layer several times (multiple patterning) lets these tools print features that would otherwise need EUV, and Chinese fabs have used that route toward the advanced nodes on which domestic AI accelerators are made, ``printing its way'' toward self-sufficiency in the AEI authors' phrase \citep{aei2026lithography}. Customers in China accounted for 26.3 percent of ASML's total net sales in 2023, 36.1 percent in 2024 and 29.1 percent in 2025 \citep{asml2026annual}. Each sale also starts a servicing relationship. Installed-base management (service, upgrades and field options) made up 25.1 percent of ASML's 2025 sales \citep{asml2026annual}, and \citet{aei2026lithography} estimate that several hundred ASML immersion tools run in China, each needing specialized maintenance about every six months, often from ASML staff on site. \Cref{sec:holdup} takes up what interrupting such service would cost.

\subsection{A nested chokepoint}

ASML is a Dutch company, but its EUV machine depends on suppliers and staff in Germany and the United States. Carl Zeiss SMT is its sole supplier of critical optics, without which ASML says it ``would effectively cease to be able to conduct our business'' \citep{asml2026annual}. At the end of 2025 ASML had 8,242 employees in the United States, 18.7 percent of its workforce \citep{asml2026annual}, and US-incorporated subsidiaries that include Cymer, LLC, the San Diego maker of its EUV light sources \citep{asml2026subsidiaries}. Because the Export Administration Regulations count any juridical person organized under US law and any person in the United States as a ``U.S. person'' \citep{ecfr2026usperson}, these entities and their staff fall under US rules that restrict support for advanced-node fabs in China \citep{asml2026annual}.

Washington also reaches the machines. An October 2023 rule set the de minimis threshold of US-origin content at zero for certain immersion tools bound for China \citep{ecfr7344, bis2023sme}. A rule effective on 2 December 2024 added a foreign direct product (FDP) rule and a de minimis rule specific to semiconductor manufacturing equipment (SME). Its new Supplement No.~4 to Part 742 lists 33 countries: 25 EU members (all but Cyprus and Malta), Australia, Canada, Iceland, Japan, New Zealand, Norway, Switzerland and the United Kingdom, but not South Korea or Taiwan. The rule's new country-wide licence requirements do not apply to items caught only by the SME FDP or SME de minimis rule when an entity located in a listed country exports them from abroad, unless that entity is headquartered in Country Group D:5 or Macau \citep{bis2024fdpr}. BIS gave its reason: the governments listed ``are members of multilateral export control regimes \ldots{} and have the authority to control key SME items of concern'' \citep{bis2024fdpr}. BIS can amend the list by rule. Since the exclusion is conditioned on the listed government's own capacity to control the items, we code it as a threat left unexercised, equivalently a reward for self-binding, and not as a transfer (\cref{sec:coding}).

\subsection{Europe's dependencies}

\paragraph{Security.} Europe relies on the United States for nuclear deterrence, and Bruegel and the Kiel Institute estimate that defending Europe without US support would take about EUR 250 billion a year in additional conventional spending \citep{burilkov2025defending}. The guarantee is graduated. Its core is protected by statute: Section 1250A of the FY2024 National Defense Authorization Act bars withdrawal from the North Atlantic Treaty without the consent of two-thirds of the Senate or an Act of Congress (22 U.S.C. \S~1928f). % TODO(bib): no refs.bib key for Section 1250A; source notes/review/security.md row 43.
Posture, by contrast, is cheap to move at the margin. On 1 May 2026, after Chancellor Merz said the ``Americans clearly have no strategy'' on Iran, Defense Secretary Hegseth ordered about 5,000 troops out of Germany, and a Pentagon official cited ``recent German rhetoric'' \citep{martinez2026germany}. Later that month the US brigade combat teams based in Europe were cut from four to three, % TODO(bib): no refs.bib key for the 19 May 2026 Washington Examiner report; source notes/review/security.md row 57.
and in September the Pentagon was weighing a drawdown of 25,000 or more troops \citep{nbc2026pentagon}. Dependence also runs the other way. Spain was already refusing US use of jointly operated bases for the Iran war when, on 30 March 2026, it closed its airspace to the US aircraft involved, and Secretary of State Rubio said ``all of it's going to have to be reexamined'' \citep{roque2026spain}. Washington needs European access, and a European government can deny it. \Cref{sec:conditionality} interprets these episodes.

\paragraph{Frontier AI.} Washington has treated access to advanced AI chips as an instrument of policy. The AI Diffusion Rule of January 2025 put ten EU members in its favoured group and left the other seventeen to country allocations \citep{bis2025diffusion}. On 13 May 2025, before its compliance date, BIS announced its rescission, saying it would have ``undermined U.S. diplomatic relations with dozens of countries by downgrading them to second-tier status.'' % TODO(bib): key bis2025rescission (notes/review/lit_ai.md) is not in refs.bib; source notes/v2/facts_record.md item 11.3.
In the August 2025 joint statement on trade the EU stated its intention to buy at least \$40 billion of US AI chips and to adopt technology-security requirements aligned with those of the United States, and Washington said it would facilitate those exports once the requirements were in place \citep{whitehouse2025jointstatement}. The National Security Strategy released in December 2025 says Washington stands ready to help, ``potentially through more favorable treatment on commercial matters, technology sharing, and defense procurement'', countries that take more responsibility for their regional security and ``align their export controls with ours'' \citep{whitehouse2025nss}.

\paragraph{China.} By customer location, China was ASML's largest market in 2025 \citep{asml2026annual}. Beijing has also built instruments aimed at those who restrict it. Its licensing regime of 4 April 2025 for seven medium and heavy rare earths and magnets containing them remains in force \citep{mofcom2025rareearth, benchmark2026rareearth}. The broader, extraterritorial controls of October 2025 \citep{mayerbrown2025rareearth} were suspended after the Busan summit of 30 October 2025 until 10 November 2026 \citep{whitehouse2025busan, mofcom2025pause}. In September 2026 the two governments agreed to extend the arrangement that paused them to 10 January 2027, although no legal act extending the suspension itself had appeared by late October \citep{mofcom2026extension}. State Council Decree 834 of April 2026 authorizes countermeasures against foreign firms that cut off normal trade with Chinese entities \citep{statecouncil2026supplychain}, and Decree 835 of 13 April 2026 creates a Malicious Entity List, prohibition orders, private damages suits and fines for those who implement foreign extraterritorial measures \citep{reedsmith2026decree}. Its first application was aimed at Brussels: on 15 May 2026 China's Ministry of Justice determined that the Commission's investigative measures in its Foreign Subsidies Regulation probe of Nuctech ``constitute improper extraterritorial jurisdiction measures'' and barred anyone from implementing them \citep{cleary2026nuctech, mofcom2026nuctech}. Beijing has also executed a targeted counter-threat against a Dutch step taken under US pressure, in the Nexperia case (\cref{sec:record}).

\subsection{Who decides in Europe}

Under Article 12(2) of the EU dual-use regulation, export licences are granted by the competent authority of the member state where the exporter is established \citep{eu2021dualuse}. Other member states have only non-binding consultation rights, so licences for ASML's shipments to China are a Dutch decision. The US-facing direction is collective. Union general export authorisation EU001 covers exports of listed dual-use items to the United States and eight other destinations, and a member state may bar an exporter from it only on ``reasonable suspicion about the exporter's ability to comply'' (Article 12(7)) \citep{eu2021dualuse}. Tools left on the Dutch national list still need a Dutch licence to leave the EU \citep{asml2026annual}, but EUV and the most advanced immersion tools ship to US fabs under EU001. Turning them against Washington would take an EU decision, under the Anti-Coercion Instrument \citep{eu2023aci} or by amending EU001 (\cref{sec:council}). Ownership is guarded at two levels. ASML's independent preference-share foundation can acquire preference shares that would halve the voting power of ordinary shares when, in its board's view, the interests of ASML or its stakeholders are at stake \citep{asml2026annual}. The Dutch Vifo Act, in force since 1 June 2023, requires notification of acquisitions that reach 10 percent of the votes in firms holding ``very sensitive'' technology, a category whose definition covers machines for chip manufacturing \citep{nl2022vifo, nl2023sensitief}.

The controls themselves arrived in steps, and from 2023 each step followed a US move. In 2019 the Dutch government let a licence for an EUV shipment to China lapse, reportedly after US lobbying \citep{nltimes2020pressured}. After the US rules of October 2022, it published national licensing for the most advanced immersion tools (NXT:2000i and later) on 30 June 2023, in force from 1 September 2023 \citep{govnl2023export}, and at the end of 2023 it partly revoked a licence covering a small number of Chinese customers \citep{dutchnews2024licence}. After the US rule of October 2023, it extended licensing from 7 September 2024 to the older NXT:1970i and NXT:1980i, with their specially designed parts, software and technology, so that ASML now applied to The Hague rather than Washington for these shipments \citep{asml2024license}. After the December 2024 rule, it published controls on metrology and inspection systems on 15 January 2025, in force from 1 April 2025 \citep{asml2026annual}. On 15 November 2025 Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/2003 put the most advanced immersion tools on the EU list. ASML reported that this ``did not mean a change'' because the tools already needed a Dutch licence, and the licence decision stayed in The Hague \citep{asml2026annual, eu2021dualuse}. % TODO(bib): key eu2025dualuse (notes/review/asml_facts.md) is not in refs.bib.
Servicing of the installed base in China remained permitted at least into mid-2026 \citep{zakaria2026match}. The MATCH Act, introduced in Congress in April 2026 and not enacted by late October, would ban immersion-tool sales anywhere in China and servicing at listed Chinese fabs, giving allies 150 days to align before Washington acted alone \citep{baumgartner2026match, hr8170}. The Dutch government told parliament that it opposed the bill's extraterritorial reach \citep{tk2026match}, and the trade minister said it ``could potentially put our national security in American hands'' \citep{nltimes2026irritated}. \Cref{sec:record} reads this sequence through the model.
